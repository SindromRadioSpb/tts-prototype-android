## Findings

- **FACT:** live system supports a reading→tap/mark→FSRS due→contextual review loop, but lacks external activation, W1/W4, WTP and unified first-run evidence.
- **BLOCKER:** keep public positioning reading/outcome-led; authentic content + AI + saved words is already claimed by products such as [Readlang](https://readlang.com/landing-page) and [LingQ](https://www.lingq.com/en/?landing=false).
- **MAJOR:** agency is product-wide: recommended default, why, alternatives, not now, undo/annul, deterministic-only and explicit privacy tier.
- **MAJOR:** accessibility requires keyboard/screen-reader/RTL/reflow/contrast/reduced-motion/target-size and modality substitution gates; code presence alone is not conformance.
- **PROPOSAL:** primary JTBD is helping an A2–B2 learner understand self-chosen Hebrew, preserve context and remember/use difficulties without rebuilding them across five tools.

Three bets: Context-Preserving Reading Loop; One Explainable Next Action; Learner-Controlled Scaffold Fading. Each is killed by absent delayed-transfer lift, reading displacement, hint rebound, accessibility harm or poor cost.

Tests: curated 90-second Room-first activation; deterministic due vs recommender; manual vs recommended modality; fixed vs adaptive scaffold; reading-led vs AI/OS positioning; keyboard/screen-reader/zoom/RTL usability.

Rejected: generic chat, unlimited exercises, streak optimization, OS-first marketing, hidden adaptive UI, speaking certification and teacher dashboard. Disagreement: this lens permits thin M-B after measured learner value; R-H funds only S1. Gap: no interviews/cohort baseline were executed.
