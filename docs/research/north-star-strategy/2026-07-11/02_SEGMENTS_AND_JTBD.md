## Selection

**PROPOSAL — primary:** independent A2–B2 Hebrew learner who already tries to read real texts and currently combines a translator/dictionary with Anki or notes. The wedge fits shipped strengths, frequency is high, differentiation is legible, and the user can supply valuable material without waiting for a full curriculum.

**Secondary:** new immigrant / ulpan learner handling real everyday messages, served through a guided Studio first-use path and curated starter texts. This segment has higher urgency but needs stronger scaffolding and simplicity.

**Future:** advanced productive-language learner; teacher/author; professional verticals. Speaking/writing and institutional workflows require stronger grader, role, privacy, and curriculum evidence.

**Do not serve now:** absolute beginners expecting a complete Hebrew course; schools requiring teacher dashboards; general translation-only users; generic AI-chat seekers; certification/high-stakes assessment users.

## Primary JTBD and switching hypothesis

“When I meet Hebrew I genuinely want or need to understand, help me read it now and turn the difficult parts into knowledge I can recognize and use later—without rebuilding the context in five tools.”

Activation hypothesis: a learner who completes one authentic fragment, resolves at least one difficulty, and sees a scheduled contextual return within the first session is more likely to return in week 1 than a learner who merely creates cards.

## Facts, inferences, assumptions, unresolved questions

**FACT:** Reading, enrichment, memory, and mentor continuity exist; external cohort research is not present. **INFERENCE:** independent real-text learners best match current readiness. **ASSUMPTIONS:** A2–B2 can navigate authentic material with scaffolding; they will grant selective cloud consent for continuity. **UNRESOLVED:** segment size, WTP, Hebrew/Russian/English channel mix, acquisition source, first-text availability, and measurable switching from Anki/LingQ/chat.
