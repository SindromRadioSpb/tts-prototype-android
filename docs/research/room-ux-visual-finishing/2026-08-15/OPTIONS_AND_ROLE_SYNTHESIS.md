## A/B/C comparison

| Criterion | A — conservative normalization | B — editorial reading system | C — utility modernization |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product character | keeps current mixed identity | strongest LinguistPro reading/learning identity | coherent but generic |
| Regression risk | lowest | moderate and controllable with staged adoption | highest because density/layout pressure expands scope |
| Emoji debt | reduces repeated controls/status | replaces identity and repeated semantics coherently | replaces most, potentially over-systematizes |
| RU/EN/HE typography | fixes bugs, limited optical gain | explicit script roles and editorial hierarchy | consistent sans, weaker literary parity |
| State maturity | normalizes colors/copy | quiet shared anatomy with strong contextual copy | clearest operational panels, can dominate content |
| Cross-surface scale | incremental aliases | small foundations + local compositions | pushes toward component rewrite |
| Real owner fixtures | safe but leaves 1920/header and long-title weaknesses | best balance on Ben/My Texts/380/Studio | excels in dense My Texts, weakens Ben/L0 |
| Backward compatibility | best | good with aliases/text fallback | weakest |

Recommended: **B**, implemented with A's additive token aliases and serialized release discipline.

## External constraints applied

- WCAG 2.2: 4.5:1 text, 3:1 meaningful graphics/UI state, 24×24 minimum target or spacing exception, visible/non-obscured focus, 200%/reflow and text-spacing resilience.
- W3C i18n: language and direction are separate; use `lang`, `dir` and `bdi` deliberately.
- W3C Media Queries: `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` requires a less-motion equivalent, not a slower loop.
- Lucide licensing: a vendored subset is viable only with ISC/MIT notices and exact provenance.

## Why not an unbounded hybrid

“B + A + C” is not the decision. The thesis remains B: editorial calm and operational clarity. A contributes the migration method; C contributes only status rigor and scale checks. Anything that makes neutral utility chrome visually dominant belongs to Direction E2/C and is not approved by the recommended value.
